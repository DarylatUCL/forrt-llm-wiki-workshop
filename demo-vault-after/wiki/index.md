# Index

## Sources

- [[head-2015]] Head et al. (2015), The extent and consequences of p-hacking in science. Text-mined p-curves across 14 disciplines and p-curves from 12 sexual-selection meta-analyses.
- [[bishop-2016]] Bishop and Thompson (2016), Problems in using p-curve analysis and text-mining to detect rate of p-hacking and evidential value. Simulations of ghost-variable p-hacking, a power analysis of the near-bin test, and a manual audit of 30 text-mined papers.
- [[hartgerink-2017]] Hartgerink (2017), Reanalyzing Head et al. (2015): Investigating the robustness of widespread p-hacking. Reanalysis of the text-mined p-values with p = .045 and p = .05 retained and bins chosen around two-decimal reporting; no surplus below .05 remains.
- [[bruns-2016]] Bruns and Ioannidis (2016), p-Curve and p-hacking in observational research. Simulations of null effects with omitted-variable bias and a cross-country growth illustration; right-skewed p-curves without any true effect, and no peak below 0.05.
- [[wicherts-2016]] Wicherts et al. (2016), Degrees of freedom in planning, running, analyzing, and reporting psychological studies: A checklist to avoid p-hacking. A review article listing 34 researcher degrees of freedom across five phases of a study, offered as a checklist for assessing preregistrations and unregistered studies.
- [[fraser-2018]] Fraser et al. (2018), Questionable research practices in ecology and evolution. A self-report survey of 807 ecology and evolutionary biology researchers on ten questionable research practices, comparing self-reported and estimated rates of use with two published psychology surveys.
- [[agnoli-2017]] Agnoli et al. (2017), Questionable research practices among Italian research psychologists. A self-report survey of 277 Italian research psychologists (208 completing), directly replicating John et al.'s (2012) US survey of ten questionable research practices.
- [[hardwicke-2018]] Hardwicke et al. (2018), Data availability, reusability, and analytic reproducibility. A pre-/post-evaluation of a mandatory open data policy at the journal Cognition (591 articles) and, for a subset of 35 articles with reusable data, a case-study check of whether reported analyses could be reproduced (1,324 target values).
- [[vasilevsky-2017]] Vasilevsky et al. (2017), Reproducible and reusable research: Are journal data sharing policies meeting the mark? A landscape survey of the data sharing policies of 318 biomedical journals, scored on a six-level rubric and tested against journal impact factor, publishing volume and open-access status.
- [[haven-2019a]] Haven, Bouter, et al. (2019), Perceived publication pressure in Amsterdam: Survey of all disciplinary fields and academic ranks. A survey of 1,073 academic researchers measuring publication pressure with the revised Publication Pressure Questionnaire (PPQr), with Stress, Attitude and Resources subscale differences by academic rank and disciplinary field.
- [[haven-2019b]] Haven, Tijdink, et al. (2019), Perceptions of research integrity climate differ between academic ranks and disciplinary fields. A survey of 1,298 academic researchers measuring research integrity climate with the Survey of Organizational Research Climate (SOuRCe), with seven-subscale differences by academic rank and disciplinary field.
- [[gopalakrishna-2022]] Gopalakrishna et al. (2022), Prevalence of questionable research practices, research misconduct and their potential explanatory factors. The Dutch National Survey on Research Integrity (6,813 completing), with three-year prevalence of frequent engagement in 11 QRPs, randomised-response estimates of fabrication and falsification, and associations with 11 explanatory factor scales including publication pressure.
